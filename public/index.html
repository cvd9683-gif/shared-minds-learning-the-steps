How this works

You and the dancer take turns.

Your turn. Every move you play is recorded with the time you played it, to the millisecond. The dancer mirrors you as you go, so the keys feel like an instrument rather than a form. The gaps between your moves are the phrase: a pause you leave in the middle is kept exactly as you left it, and nothing is treated as the end of your turn until you hand it over.

The dancer's turn. Your phrase goes to a language model as a list of moves and the gap in milliseconds before each one — numbers and names, nothing else. It never sees the dancer, or you. It answers with the phrase it would dance back, at its own timing, and the dancer performs that on its own. It can reach for three moves you have no key for: spin, bounce and hold.

Both phrases are written out above at the same scale, so you can read one against the other: which moves, and how far apart.

Try my next phrase is the honest version of the question. The model proposed a continuation without knowing what you intended; playing what you would really have done next puts the two side by side. It is an experiment, not a score — there is no right answer to guess at.

Development details

The request goes to a small local server, which holds the API token so this page never sees it. The log records what went out, what came back, and how long it took. Setup is in the README.
